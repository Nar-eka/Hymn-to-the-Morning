## Critical Introduction to "To An Hymn to the Morning"

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Phillis Wheatley Peters’ poem, “An Hymn to the Morning,” published in her 1773 book, _Poems on Various Subjects, 
Religious and Moral_, depicts a scene where the speaker gains inspiration from nature, after calling upon the nine Muses. The nine Muses being the sister goddesses 
from Greco-Roman religion and mythology, fathered by “Zeus, king of the . . . gods, and Mnemosyne,” goddess of memory (Carretta 173-4). These nine sister goddesses, 
“Calliope (epic poetry), Clio (history), Euterpe (flute-playing), Terpsichore (lyric poetry and dancing, especially choral), Erato (lyric poetry), Melpomene (tragedy), 
Thalia (comedy), Polyhymnia (hymns and pantomime), and Urania (astronomy),” provide inspiration within the “arts, [sciences], and literature,” and are led by 
“Phoebus Apollo, god of the sun . . . music and poetry” (174). This moment of artistic euphoria is short lived, however, as the speaker’s creative connection 
to the natural world is dictated by the passage of time and thus interrupted when the increasing intensity of the sun’s rays, as the morning arrives, touches the 
speaker’s skin. 



## An Hymn to the Morning.
MAECENAS, you, beneath the myrtle shade, {% include essay/feature/aside.html text="Here is a demo note." %}
<br>Read o’er what poets sung, and shepherds play’d.
<br>What felt those poets but you feel the same?
<br>Does not your soul possess the sacred flame?
<br>Their noble strains your equal genius shares
<br>In softer language, and diviner airs.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ATTEND my lays, ye ever honour'd nine,  
Assist my labours, and my strains refine;  
In smoothest numbers pour the notes along,  
For bright _Aurora_ now demands my song.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;_Aurora_ hail, and all the thousand dies,  
Which deck thy progress through the vaulted skies:  
The mourn awakes, and wide extends her rays,  
On ev'ry leaf the gentle zephyr plays;  
Harmonious lays the feather'd race resume,  
Dart the bright eye, and shake the painted plume.
